    How to read this

    This is your real pickleball history from DUPR, sliced up so you can see what’s actually going on — who you beat, who beats you, and whether you’re playing better or worse than your rating says you should. Here’s what everything means.

    The tabs at the top

    OverviewYour last session first, then your record, headline numbers, rating over time with its all-time range, and highlights: your upset record and standout matches.
    TrendsThe deep dives: recent form, performance by event (with tournament results) and by date, matchup strength, close games, streaks, and rating-adjusted splits (time of day, days off, playing up or down a level).
    Opponents & PartnersWho you play best and worst with (partners) and against (opponents), vs what the ratings expect — plus a matchup predictor. The switch at the top flips every card between partners and opponents.
    MatchesYour full match log, filterable and sortable, with quick chips for wins, losses, upset wins and given-up losses.
    ?The button at the top opens this page.

    Period (All time · 12 mo · 60 days · Custom… for a year or a date range) and Filters (only tournaments, one partner, and so on) sit in the bar at the top and apply to every tab. When either is on, the bar has a gold outline and a ✕ Clear button. A few things ignore them on purpose:

    • Always your latest: Last session, Recent rating changes (Trends), the Overview sparklines, the all-time rating range, and the rating goal and matchup predictor.
    • Chart only: the Rating Over Time zoom (3M / 6M / 1Y…) changes the chart, not the numbers.

    Only doubles matches are counted; any singles in your DUPR history are left out.

    The numbers

    DUPRYour official rating from DUPR. Higher is stronger.
    Win %The share of matches you won.
    Points won %Of all the points played, how many you won. A truer measure of how close things were than win–loss alone. It pools every point, so a game to 15 counts a little more than a game to 11.
    MarginOn average, how many points you win or lose a game by — rescaled to an 11-point game (games to 15 and 21 are scaled down) so an 11–9 and a 15–12 compare fairly. It's an average of games. +2.0 means you typically win by about 2; −2.0 means you lose by about 2. A single game swings a lot, typically by about 5 points either way of what the ratings expect, so one night is never a trend.
    Playing atThe DUPR your recent results were worth: the rating at which the ratings would have expected exactly the wins you got over your last 20 matches. It comes with a range, because 20 matches is still noisy. When it sits above your DUPR for a while, your DUPR has room to rise.
    DUPR changeHow much a match (or a group of matches) moved your rating — DUPR’s own number, shown as a rating amount like +0.013. It already takes the strength of everyone on court into account.
    Rating went up How often your rating went up after a match. You can lose a match and still gain rating if you played a strong team closely, and that counts here. Matches with no rating change (0.000) are left out.
    Avg opponentThe average DUPR of the opponents in that slice, going into each match. Shows how tough your schedule was.

    Expected vs actual — the big idea

    Because every player has a rating, the app can work out how you should do in a match, then compare it with what really happened. This is what the Opponents & Partners tab is built on.

    Expected Win% / W–LWhat the ratings say should happen when you play these people.
    Wins vs expectedYour real wins minus the wins the ratings predicted. +2 = you won two more than expected (you punch above your rating here). −2 = two fewer (something isn’t clicking).
    Margin vs expectedThe same idea for the score: do you win (or lose) by more or less than the ratings say you should?
    This is why a partner can show a winning record but still read as “below expected” — if you should have won even more given how the ratings stacked up. It’s how the tab finds the people you should be winning with, but aren’t.

    How much to trust a row

    A few good or bad nights can make any split look dramatic. Two things help you tell a real pattern from a fluke:

    SignalWeighs your wins and your points against what the ratings expected; the arrow says which way (↑ better, ↓ worse). Clear ●●● with this many matches, a gap this big rarely happens by chance. Still check it against what you see on court. Likely ●●○ worth watching: on its own it would be unusual, but in a long list one or two like it turn up by chance. Leaning ●○○ a direction only, well within luck so far. Too early ○○○ within the range of normal chance: don’t read into it. The “vs expected” numbers are only coloured for Likely and Clear. Each table says how many of each to expect from luck alone. Matches entered into DUPR weeks after they were played are left out, since their ratings are from the wrong time.
    Effect (DUPR)The same pattern in DUPR points: how much better or worse your team plays than its ratings with (or against) someone, with a likely range. People you’ve met only a few times are pulled toward 0, so a 4–0 against a stranger can’t top the list.
    Small samplesAnyone you’ve played fewer than 4 times (or fewer than 10 games, in the games table) is listed separately and greyed out. One result swings these a lot, so treat them as early hints, not conclusions.

    The charts and the rating line

    Rating Over Time plots your DUPR after each day of play. The 3M / 6M / 1Y / All pills zoom the time range.

    Form (on Trends) smooths out some of the noise: each dot is your last 20 matches rolled together. When the line sits above the dashed line, you’ve been doing better than your own average lately. Twenty matches is still a small sample, so read the overall direction, not single bumps.

    DUPR at the time / Hindsight (the switch on Performance by Matchup Strength, which also sets the ratings shown in the match log): “at the time” uses everyone’s rating going into each match. “Hindsight” keeps your rating from then but uses everyone else’s rating today, which shows who you were really playing.

    Looking up other players

    From another player’s DUPR page you can pull in their history, or grab your most recent opponents and partners in one go, and see all these same breakdowns for them — handy for scouting before a match. Anyone you pull in is kept on this device only, under the 👁 Viewing bar at the top, and never mixed into your own numbers.

    The one thing to remember: a good loss against strong players can still raise your rating, and a lucky win over weak ones may not. That’s why “vs expected” tells you more than your win–loss record alone.
